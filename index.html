How to Make a Tier List That Actually Makes Sense

Learn how to build a clear, useful, and visually appealing tier list for games, movies, food, sports, and almost anything else you want to rank.

Ranking things sounds easy until you have twenty, thirty, or even a hundred choices sitting in front of you. Suddenly, deciding what deserves the top spot becomes much harder. That is where a visual ranking system can help. If you want to turn your ideas into an organized chart, a tier list maker provides a simple way to arrange items, customize levels, and create a ranking that is easy to understand and share.

What Is a Tier List?

A tier list is a visual ranking system that groups items into different levels instead of forcing every item into an exact numerical position.

A basic ranking might use familiar levels such as:

  • S Tier – Exceptional choices
  • A Tier – Very strong choices
  • B Tier – Good overall
  • C Tier – Average choices
  • D Tier – Below average
  • F Tier – Usually the weakest choices

The important idea is that items in the same tier are generally considered similar in quality or preference. You do not have to decide whether something is exactly number seven or number eight.

Why Are Tier Lists So Popular?

One reason tier lists work so well is their simplicity. Someone can look at a finished chart and understand the general ranking within seconds.

The format is flexible enough to work across many different subjects. People can create rankings for:

  • Video games and characters
  • Anime and manga
  • Movies and television shows
  • Sports teams and players
  • Music and artists
  • Food and restaurants
  • Technology and brands
  • Travel destinations

How to Create a Tier List From Scratch

Creating a useful tier list does not need to be complicated. The key is to start with a clear subject and a manageable collection of items.

1

Choose What You Want to Rank

Start with one specific topic. A focused subject makes the finished chart easier to understand.

2

Gather Your Items

Decide which items belong on the chart. Use images for visual subjects or text when images are unnecessary.

3

Decide What Your Tiers Mean

You do not have to use S through F. Create labels that make sense for your particular topic.

4

Rank the Items

Move each item into the tier that best represents your chosen criteria and keep your standard consistent.

How to Make Your Tier List More Useful

A visually attractive chart is nice, but a thoughtful ranking is more valuable. Before placing an item, consider the criteria that matter for your particular topic.

Useful Ranking Criteria

For example, when ranking games, you might consider:

  • Gameplay
  • Replay value
  • Variety
  • Accessibility
  • Overall enjoyment

If you are ranking food, your criteria might include taste, variety, value, presentation, and personal preference. The important part is applying the same general standard throughout the ranking.

Customize the Chart to Match Your Topic

A good tier list should not feel like a fixed template. Customization lets you make the chart fit the subject instead of forcing your idea into a predefined structure.

  • Use three tiers for a simple personal ranking.
  • Add more tiers for detailed comparisons.
  • Choose labels that clearly explain each level.
  • Keep the design readable rather than overcrowded.

Using Images or Text

The best format depends on what you are ranking. Image-based lists work particularly well for subjects such as characters, food, logos, games, and movie posters.

Text-based lists can be more practical when ranking books, names, programming languages, places, concepts, or other items without obvious images.

How to Make a Tier List on a Phone

You do not always need a computer to create a ranking chart. A browser-based tool can make the process convenient on smaller screens as well.

  1. Keep item names short and readable.
  2. Avoid placing too many items into one row.
  3. Use touch controls carefully when moving items.
  4. Check the finished chart before saving it.
  5. Choose a layout that remains readable on mobile.

How to Share Your Finished Tier List

Once your ranking is complete, you can turn it into something other people can view and discuss.

An image is useful for social media posts, group chats, forums, and community discussions because people can see the ranking immediately. A shareable version can also make it easier for others to participate in the conversation and create their own rankings.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even a simple tier list can become confusing if it is poorly organized. Keep these common mistakes in mind:

  • Adding too many unnecessary tiers
  • Using unclear tier names
  • Ranking items without consistent criteria
  • Cramming too many items into a small chart
  • Mixing unrelated subjects without explanation
  • Making the design more complicated than necessary
  • Forgetting to check the final chart before sharing

The goal is not to create the most complicated chart. The goal is to communicate your ranking clearly.
